Design process

From research and mockups to AI prototypes you can actually test

Overview

Our design process is constantly evolving. The traditional approach, with research and user interviews up front followed by rounds of static mockups, has been replaced by rapid prototyping with AI and testing on working prototypes. Instead of guessing how something will feel from a set of screens, we build a version people can click through, put it in front of users and stakeholders early, and learn from how they actually use it.

Challenge

The old process worked, but it was slow. We started with research and interviews, turned them into wireframes and then high-fidelity designs, reviewed them within the team, then with product managers and our data scientists, and finally with engineering to estimate the work and prepare the handoff. By the time anything reached a real user, a lot of time had passed. And a static mockup never really tells you how something feels to use, especially with the dense, data-heavy interfaces we build, where the details only show up once you're working with real interactions and realistic data.

My role

I've been pushing the team to bring AI into every stage of the work: analysing data and feedback, writing specs, and above all building interactive prototypes. A designer can now go from an idea to something working in a fraction of the time it used to take, and we test that instead of a picture of it. Research hasn't disappeared, it just happens differently. Rather than long interview rounds before anything exists, we learn by putting something real in front of people and watching what they do with it, which gives us more honest and more useful feedback.

Design reviews are still at the heart of how we work, but now we review working prototypes together with product and engineering. That makes conversations about scope and feasibility much more concrete, because everyone is looking at the same thing. It also changes what a designer's job is. Less time pushing pixels and preparing handoff files, more time deciding what's worth building and why. I want the team to be decision-makers who use AI well, not just makers.

What's next

The tools change every few months, so the process will keep changing with them. What I want to hold on to is the habit of questioning how we work, rather than treating any process as finished.
